# Post-MVP Roadmap: ASPFullStackBMAD Product Catalog

Status: Proposed · Date: 2026-08-22 · Author: Prasadmallavalli · Satisfies: Story 5.2 (FR-10's roadmap requirement)

Three horizons, each grounded in a real, already-identified gap from this build (deferred-work.md, the scaling design doc, and Story 5.1's postmortem) — not a generic "what SaaS apps usually add next" list. Each horizon states the user- or business-facing reason first, not just a feature name, then the technical work that closes it — each `## Horizon` heading below opens with a `**Why:**` paragraph for exactly this reason.

Each horizon lists an explicit Owner and Definition of done, matching this project's own convention of naming an owner per open item (`deferred-work.md`, the Story 5.1 postmortem's follow-up actions).

## Horizon 1 (near-term): Make the catalog usable past a demo-sized dataset

**Why:** the product listing works today because it's tested with a handful of rows. A real catalog — even a small business's, let alone anything larger — has hundreds or thousands of products. Past that point, users can't find what they're looking for by scrolling an unbounded, unsearched list, and every page load ships the entire catalog over the wire whether the user needed row 4 or row 400. This isn't a hypothetical scaling concern; it's the difference between a catalog someone can actually shop and one they abandon. The identical gap exists on `GET /api/categories` too (`CategoryRepository.GetAllAsync` has the same unbounded, unordered shape) — smaller today since categories are few, but the same design applies once it isn't.

**What closes it:** keyset pagination, category filtering, and (net-new, not yet designed) search-by-name on `GET /api/products`, plus the equivalent pagination fix for `GET /api/categories`. The design for the Product endpoint already exists in [`docs/design/scaling-product-list-pagination.md`](../design/scaling-product-list-pagination.md) — but that design doc is honest that implementing it is real, non-trivial scope of its own: it means renegotiating Story 3.2's frozen spec, a bigger frontend change than the backend side (new cursor/accumulated-item/"has more" state, a redefinition of what "refresh" means after a delete, accessible load-more announcements), and new test surface. This horizon is "the design work is done," not "the implementation is small." Search-by-name and the Category-endpoint twin fix are both net-new, not yet designed. Once pagination ships, the design doc's own "Out of scope" section already flags that `limit` clamping alone doesn't stop a client from walking the whole catalog via repeated cursor calls — closing that fully needs the same rate limiting this roadmap's Horizon 2 adds for the auth endpoints, applied here too, not treated as an auth-only concern.

**Owner:** Prasadmallavalli. **Definition of done:** `GET /api/products`/`GET /api/categories` both paginated and indexed per the design doc, search-by-name shipped, and a stated target (e.g., page-load payload stays under a fixed size regardless of catalog row count) verified against a seeded dataset larger than what manual testing currently uses.

## Horizon 2 (mid-term): Close the account-security gaps that block a real launch

**Why:** the current auth flow is honest about being a demo, not because it's deliberately simplified, but because closing these specific gaps was consistently out of scope for the stories that touched auth (see `deferred-work.md`, Stories 2.1/2.2/2.3). Before this could be a real product anyone signs up for, the absence of these controls stops being an acceptable MVP tradeoff and starts being a real liability: automated account creation, credential-stuffing with no throttling, no way to end a session early, no email verification step, and — a direct hit against "real product" framing specifically — no *documented plan* exists for how a production JWT signing key would be supplied, protected, or rotated. This is a separate, still-open gap from the specific leaked-key incident the code review checklist's Item 10 covers: that incident (a real credential committed in plaintext) was found, rotated, and scrubbed from git history — closed. What's still open is that closing that one incident didn't produce an actual secrets-management *system* — the current key still lives in a plaintext local config file, with no process for what happens the next time a key needs rotating on purpose, not by accident. None of this is exotic — it's the baseline a user (and a security-conscious engineer) expects from *any* account system, and its absence is the kind of thing that erodes trust fast if discovered rather than fixed proactively.

**What closes it:** rate limiting/throttling on `POST /api/auth/register` and `POST /api/auth/login` (already logged, [`deferred-work.md`](../../_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/deferred-work.md) Stories 2.1/2.2), an email-verification flow (`IsEmailConfirmed` + confirmation token, already logged as absent), a logout endpoint that actually clears the session cookie (already logged as absent), a production secrets-management plan for the JWT signing key (already logged, Story 2.2 section), and — net-new, not yet designed anywhere — a password-reset flow, since none exists at all today. Also real and already logged, bundled into this horizon rather than listed as their own line items: a minimum password-length/complexity policy (Story 2.1), the email-enumeration timing side-channel present on both register and login (Stories 2.1/2.2, also [checklist](../review/code-review-checklist.md) Item 9), no JWT `jti`/revocation mechanism (Story 2.2), the session cookie's lifetime not being tied to the JWT's own expiry (Story 2.3 post-review entry), and — surfaced after this incident was closed, not before — no pre-commit hook or secret scanner exists to catch the *next* accidental credential commit before it happens (see the [hiring question set](hiring-question-set.md) Question 8's follow-up).

**Also real, deliberately not its own horizon:** accessibility is the single most frequently repeated gap category in `deferred-work.md` — missing landmarks/`aria-busy` (Story 3.2), no focus management or `aria-invalid`/`aria-describedby` on the login/product forms (the login-form prerequisite, Stories 3.3/3.4), no accessible name or destructive styling on Delete (Story 3.5). It doesn't get a horizon of its own here because it's cross-cutting rather than a single feature, but a real roadmap execution plan would need to fold an accessibility pass across all three horizons above rather than treat it as separately deferrable.

**Owner:** Prasadmallavalli. **Definition of done:** every gap named above closed and re-verified against the same manual end-to-end flow (register → login → mutate → logout) this project already uses for its own regression checks; production secrets sourced from something other than a tracked file.

## Horizon 3 (longer-term): Operate it like a production service, not a repo that happens to run

**Why:** Story 5.1's own postmortem found a gap none of Epics 1-4's reviews had surfaced: there is no monitoring or alerting for this API at all. Combined with the still-open DI-lifetime regression-test gap (logged independently in `deferred-work.md`'s Story 1.5 section, `ADR-006`'s Consequences, and checklist Item 3 — three sources before the postmortem's own mention makes a fourth) and the complete absence of CI (`deferred-work.md`, Story 1.6 section — no `.github/workflows` exists for either project), the honest state is that this system has no automated way to notice it broke, no automated way to stop a known regression from shipping, and no automated way to verify a change is safe before it merges. For a real team, this horizon is what turns "code that works when I run it locally" into "a service other people can depend on" — it's infrastructure investment, not a user-facing feature, but it's the reason an incident like Story 5.1's postmortem would actually get caught in minutes instead of requiring someone to notice by hand.

**What closes it:** a CI pipeline running `dotnet build`/`dotnet test` on every change (closes the already-logged Story 1.6 gap directly) and `npm run build`/`npm test` alongside it (the same "no `.github/workflows` for either project" gap, though the frontend half of this isn't logged against a specific story the way the backend half is — stated here rather than overclaiming a citation that doesn't fully exist yet); a DI-container lifetime assertion or regression test guarding the specific Story 1.5 misregistration class (already logged: [`deferred-work.md`](../../_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/deferred-work.md) Story 1.5 section, [ADR-006](../adr/006-scoped-di-lifetimes.md)'s Consequences); basic error-rate alerting on the API (closes [the postmortem](postmortem-di-captive-dependency.md)'s own newly-surfaced finding); pinning `Microsoft.OpenApi` off 2.0.0, which carries a known high-severity advisory (GHSA-v5pm-xwqc-g5wc) surfaced as a build warning since Story 1.1 (`deferred-work.md`, Story 1.5 section); and closing the check-then-act races ([checklist](../review/code-review-checklist.md) Item 5 — three still-open instances across Category delete and Product/User creation, currently surfacing as raw 500s instead of clean 409/400s) plus adding the missing controller-level HTTP-status-mapping tests (checklist Item 11, where "every existing test would still pass if either mapping were inverted or dropped") — both squarely about being a service other people can depend on, not a Horizon 1/2 concern.

**On sizing these three horizons:** [the estimation-calibration note](estimation-calibration-note.md) — this roadmap's own sibling from the same originally-combined planning session — concludes that "a future estimate for this kind of work should budget review cycles per artifact, not hours per topic." None of the Definitions of done above name a calendar target for exactly that reason: this project's own evidence is that hour estimates for this kind of work haven't held up, so none are asserted here either.

**Owner:** Prasadmallavalli. **Definition of done:** a green CI badge on every merge, a DI-lifetime regression test that fails on a deliberate revert to `AddSingleton` (proving it would have caught Story 1.5), and a fired test alert confirming the error-rate monitoring actually works, not just exists on paper.

## Sequencing note

These horizons are not strictly ordered by calendar time so much as by "what breaks first if ignored." Horizon 1 is the one a real user notices immediately (an unusable catalog). Horizon 2 is the one that becomes a liability the moment this stops being a portfolio demo and starts being reachable by real strangers. Horizon 3 is the one that determines how fast the *next* incident like Story 5.1's gets caught and fixed — least visible to a user on day one, most consequential the first time something goes wrong in production.

**The tension worth naming rather than hiding:** ranking Horizon 3 last by user-visibility is not the same as saying it should be *built* last. A CI pipeline and the DI-lifetime regression test are exactly the kind of investment that reduces delivery risk for Horizons 1 and 2 — shipping pagination or a password-reset flow with no CI is riskier than shipping either with it in place first. A real prioritization call would weigh that against Horizon 3's low user-visibility, not just read this roadmap's ordering as a build sequence.
